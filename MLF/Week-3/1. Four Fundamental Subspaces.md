# Column Space C(A)
$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} | & |  & | \\ u_1, & u_2 & u_n\\ | & | & | \end{bmatrix}
$$
- $C(A) =$ span $(u_1, u_2, \dots , u_n)$ = { linear combinations of the vectors $u_1, u_2, \dots u_n$}
 - Solving $Ax=b$
	 - For what $b$ does $Ax = b$ have a solution?
		 - $b \in C(A)$
 - Let's look at an example
 - $$ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 & 3 \\4 & 1& 5\end{bmatrix} $$
 - $Ax = b$ is a solvable for $b \in C(A)$
 - Does every vector in $\mathbb{R}^4$ belong to $C(A)?$
	 - Some vectors in $\mathbb{R}^4$ are not in $C(A)$, because $Ax = b$ is "4 equations in 3 unknowns"
 - For the example above, $Col3 = Col1 + Col2$
	 - So $C(A)$ = span ($Col1 + Col2$)
	 - $C(A)$ is a two dimensional subspace of $\mathbb{R}^4$
# Null Space N(A)
- $N(A) = \{x | Ax = 0\}$
- Why is $N(A)$ a subspace?
	- $x_1, x_2 \in N(A)$
	- Then $Ax_1 = 0, Ax_2 = 0$
	- $A(x_1 + x_2) = 0$
	- $x_1 + x_2 \in N(A)$
	- $x \in N(A), \alpha$ in a scalar
	- A ($\alpha x$) = $\alpha A x$
	- $= 0$
	- So $\alpha x \in N(A)$
- hence $N(A)$ in a subspace.
- Example : 
$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1& 2 \\ 2 & 1 & 3 \\ 3 & 1 & 4 \\4 & 1 & 5\end{bmatrix}
$$
- **Find an x s.t. $Ax =0$?**
	- A linear combination of the columns of A should results in the zero vector.
	- $$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \in N(A)$$
	- So, $N(A)$ is a line in $\mathbb{R}^3$
- Remark 1 : 
	- If A is invertible, then N(A) has "zero" only, and C(A) is the whole space.
	- In the case, $Ax =b$ has a unique solution $x+A^{-1}b$
	- Else, $N(A)$ has $x_n \neq 0 \text( and ) Ax=b$ solutions are of the form 
		- $x = x_p + x_n$
		- $Ax_p=b, Ax_n =0$
- Remark 2 : 
	- Can we Gaussian elimination to find the null space of a matrix A i.e. Solve $Ax =0$
	- $$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 & 6 & 8 \\3 & 6 & 8 & 10\end{bmatrix} \to \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 2& 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 & 4 \\0 & 0 & 2 & 4\end{bmatrix} \to \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 2 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$$
		- Pivot points are 1 (in row 1) and 2 (in row 2)
		- $U_x = 0$
	- $$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 2& 2 \\ 0 & 0 &2 & 4 \\0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \\ x_4\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0\\ 0\\ \end{bmatrix}$$
	- Col 1, Col 3 $\to$ pivot columns
	- Col 2, Col 4 $\to$ free variables
	- Set $x_2 = 1, x_4 = 0 => 2x_3 + 4 x_4 = 0 => x_3 = 0$
		- $x_1 + 2x_2 + 2x_3 + 2x_4 = 0 => x_1 = -2$
		- u = $\begin{bmatrix} -2 \\1 \\0 \\0\end{bmatrix} \in N(A)$
		- Set $x_2 = 0, x_4 = 1 => 2x_3 + 4x_4 = 0 => x_3  -2$
			- $x_1 + 2x_2 + 2x_3 + 2x_4 = 0$
			- $x_1 - 4 + 2 = 0 => x_1 = 2$
		- $$v = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 0 \\ -2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \in N(A)$$
		- $N(A) = span(u, v)$
- **Rank** : Number of pivot columns, In the example above, $rank(A) = 2$
- **Nullity** : Number of free variables. In the example where, $nullity(A) = 2$
$$ Rank = dim(C(A)), \\ Nullity = dim(N(A))$$
- If A has $n$ columns, then rank + nullity = n
	- rank(A) = r
	- nullity(A) = n-r
# Row Space R(A)
- Column space of $A^T \approx \text{span of rows of A}$
	- $R(A) = C(A^T)$
- Important Fact :
	- Col rank = $dim(C(A))$
	- Row rank = $dim(R(A))$
	- Column rank = row rank
- So far, we looked at $C(A) , N(A), R(A) = C(A^T)$ 
- Left Null Space $N(A^T) = \{ y | A^Ty=0\} = \{y | y^TA = 0\}$
- For a $m \times n$ matrix $A$
$$[y_1, \dots y_m] [A] = [0, \dots ,0]$$
	- A linear combination of rows leading to zero vector.
- **Remark** 
	1. A is a $m \times n$ matrix
		- $\dim(C(A)) + \dim(N(A)) =$ number of columns of A = n
		- "r" + "n-r" = n
	2. $\dim C(A^T) + \dim(N(A^T)) =$ number of rows = m
		- r + $\dim(N(A^T)) = m => \dim(N(A^T)) = m-r$
- Example : 
	- $$ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \\ 2 & 4 & 6 & 8 \\ 3 & 6 & 8 & 10\end{bmatrix}$$
	- want $y^TA = 0$
		- $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & -1\end{bmatrix} \in N(A^T)$
- Example : 
	- $$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 3 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$$
		- m = n = 2
	- $C(A) =$ line through $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3\end{bmatrix} =>$ rank(A) = r = 1
	- $N(A) =$ line through $\begin{bmatrix} -2 \\1 \end{bmatrix} =>$ nullity (A) = n-r = 1
	- $R(A) =$ $C(A^T)$ = line through $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$ dim(R(A)) = r = 1
	- Left Null space $N(A^T)$ = line through $\begin{bmatrix} -3 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} dim(N(A^T)) = 1$
