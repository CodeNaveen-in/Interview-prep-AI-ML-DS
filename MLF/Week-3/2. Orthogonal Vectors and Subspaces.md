# Length of a vectors
- $|| x ||^2 = x_1^2 + x_2^2$
- $||\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}||^2 = 1^2 + 2^2 = 5$
- In general, for x = $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ . \\. \\. \\x_n\end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^n$
	- $||x||^2 = x_1^2 + x_2^2 + \dots + x_n^2$
# Orthogonal vectors
- $x \perp y \text{ if } x^Ty = 0$
- $x = \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\. \\. \\. \\x_n \end{bmatrix}, y = \begin{bmatrix} y_1 \\. \\. \\. \\y_n \end{bmatrix} \text{ then } x^Ty = \sum^{n}_{i=1}x_iy_i$
- from Pythagoras theorem : 
	- $x^Ty = 0 \to$ orthogonal to y.
- Remark : 
	1. O is orthogonal to every x.
	2. If { $v_1, \dots v_k$ } are mutually orthogonal "non-trival" set of vectors { $v_1, \dots v_k$ } is a linearly independent set.
- Why?
	- Suppose $c_1v_1 + c_2v_2 + \dots + c_kv_k = 0$
	- $v_1^T (C_1 v_1 + \dots + C_kv_k) = 0$
	- $C_1 v_1^Tv_1 = 0$ , but $||\theta_1|| \neq 0 => C_1 = 0$
- Similarly $C_i \neq 0 \forall i$
- Example : 
	1. $\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} , \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$
	2. $\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 2 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 2 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} , \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}$
# Orthonormal vectors
- {u, v} orthonormal if $u^Tv=0$ and $||u|| = ||v|| = 1$
- example : 
	- $\begin{Bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}\end{Bmatrix}$, $\begin{Bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} cos\theta \\ sin\theta \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} -sin\theta \\ cos\theta \end{bmatrix}\end{Bmatrix}$
# Orthogonal subspaces
- $U, V$ are orthogonal subspaces if
	- $x^Ty=0 \forall x\in U, y \in V$
- Examples : 
	1. {$0$} $\bot$ subspace
	2. U = $\begin{Bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} , \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\1 \\0 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}\end{Bmatrix} V = \begin{Bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 2 \\ 1\end{Bmatrix}$
		1. $U \bot V$
	3. $W = span \begin{Bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\0 \\ -2 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \end{Bmatrix}$
	4. Then $w \bot U, w \bot V$
# Orthogonality wrt four fundamental subspaces of a matrix A
- Claim: 
	- $R(A) \bot N(A)$
- Proof : $x \in N(A)$
	- $Ax = 0$ (A is m $\times$ n )
	- $$\begin{bmatrix} \text{--- row 1 ---} \\ 
  \text{--- row 2 ---} \\ 
  \vdots \\ 
  \text{--- row } n \text{ ---}
\end{bmatrix} 
\begin{bmatrix} 
  x_1 \\ 
  x_2 \\ 
  \vdots \\ 
  x_n 
\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ \vdots \\ 0\end{bmatrix}$$
	- The equation above implies $row1 \bot x, row2 \bot x, rowm \bot x$
	- Thus, any linear combination $(C_1 row1 + C_2 row2 + \dots + C_m row m) \bot x$
	- So, $R(A) \bot N(A)$
		- $C(A^T) \bot N(A)$
	- Claim 2 : $C(A \bot N(A^T)$
		- Follows claim 1
- Example : 
	- $$ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \\ 3 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$$
		- $rank(A) = dim(C(A)) = 1$
		- $x = \begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \in N(A)$ since "Col2 = 2xCol1"
		- Row space R(A) = line through $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2\end{bmatrix}$
		- Null space N(A) = line through $\begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$
		- $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = 0$ where R(A) $\bot$ N(A)
		- C(A) = line through $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\2 \\3\end{bmatrix}$
		- N($A^T$) = $y_1 + 2y_2 + 3y_3 = 0$
		- $y^TA = 0$
## A dimensional check
- $$\dim(C(A)) + \dim(N(A)) = 2$$ (number of cols)
- $$\dim(C(A^T)) + \dim(N(A^T)) = 3$$ (number of cols)
